The two desks

🚀 IPO Desk: companies that listed in roughly the last three years. Classic value tests don't work well with so little history, so these are judged by the two best-known IPO thinkers: Jay Ritter, whose decades of IPO research show what predicts long-run IPO returns, and Bill Gurley, whose "All Revenue Is Not Created Equal" is the standard test of growth-company revenue quality. There's also an IPO checklist covering lock-up, cash runway, insider ownership and valuation, plus a link to the actual SEC prospectus.

🏛 Company Desk: established businesses through five lenses: Buffett & Munger (quality), Benjamin Graham (deep value), Peter Lynch (growth at a fair price), Philip Fisher (growth quality) and Joel Greenblatt (Magic Formula).

Adding a stock

Type its ticker in a desk's search box and press Request analysis. That opens a pre-filled GitHub issue; submit it and the site adds the stock and scores it within a few minutes. (Only the repo owner and collaborators can do this. Add each other under Settings → Collaborators.) You can also edit ipos.txt or watchlist.txt directly.

The memo

Every stock gets a written memo: the business in plain words, the bull and bear case pulled from the lenses, a bear/base/bull valuation from a two-stage cash-flow model, what to watch, and what to do if you act on it. It's assembled from the numbers, so no AI key is needed. If a free Gemini key is ever added as a repo secret (GEMINI_API_KEY), an extra "analyst's take" paragraph appears too.

Honest limits

Free data covers ~4 years of financials, while these investors looked at 10+. Scores are numeric proxies for judgments like moat durability or management quality that still need a human, which is why the IPO checklist links the prospectus. IPO prices in free data are the first-day open, not the offer price. Lock-up dates are estimated at 180 days; the prospectus has the real terms. Nothing here is advice. Use it to decide what to research, then read the filings.
